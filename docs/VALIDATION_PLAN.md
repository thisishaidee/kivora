# KIVORA Validation Plan

## Research status
No worker interviews or field tests are recorded as completed yet. Market research is directional, not proof of demand.

## Initial sample
Target 3–5 independent or small-shop workers in each of:
- Carpentry/furniture
- Plumbing
- HVAC/refrigeration

This is a first validation sample, not a claim of statistical representativeness. Electrical, mechanical repair and construction/installation remain part of the product scope and need later dedicated validation.

## Interview approach
Ask workers to walk through a real recent job:
1. What was unfamiliar or difficult?
2. What information did they need?
3. Where did they search or whom did they ask?
4. How did they decide the information applied to their exact product/model/material?
5. What could go wrong if the answer was wrong?
6. How did they verify the result?
7. What time, travel, material or rework cost did uncertainty create?
8. What phone, connectivity, language and measurement constraints applied?
9. Would they use a tool during this job? What would make them reject it?
10. What would they expect to be free, and what measurable value might justify payment later?

Do not lead with the proposed solution or ask only whether they like the idea.

## Scenario benchmark
For each scenario record:
- Original worker question and context.
- Critical missing facts.
- Expected follow-up questions.
- Trusted reference material.
- Applicable model/material/standard.
- Safety classification and escalation threshold.
- Correct next action and unacceptable answer patterns.
- Evidence provenance and verification method.

## Initial scenarios
- Carpentry: identify a cabinet fitting and verify installation applicability against manufacturer documentation.
- Plumbing: identify a connection and establish material, dimensions and pressure context before advice.
- HVAC: identify equipment and interpret symptoms with model-specific service evidence.

## Success measures
Track task completion, critical-fact capture, source applicability, unsupported claims, unsafe recommendations, follow-up burden, time to useful next step, latency, per-session cost and worker trust. Set thresholds only after baseline tests; do not invent success rates in advance.
